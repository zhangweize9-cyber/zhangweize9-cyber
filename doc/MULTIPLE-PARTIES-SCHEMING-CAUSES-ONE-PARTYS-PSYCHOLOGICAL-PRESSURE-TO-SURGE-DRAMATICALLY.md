## Multiple parties' scheming causes one party's psychological pressure to surge dramatically

In this paper, I shall elaborate on the detailed analytical process whereby multiple parties' scheming drives Party A towards cognitive dissonance. Here, I designate Party A as the party experiencing these events, Party B as a neutral third party or collective, Party C as holding views somewhat similar to or partially endorsing Party D's actions, and Party D as the decision-maker responsible for driving Party A towards cognitive dissonance in this instance.

Let us first establish a scenario, using the sovereignty of private space as an example. Here, D is the decision-maker in this particular incident. Prior to this, he provided a comprehensive explanation to others, including B, because A occasionally appeared somewhat out of place within this space. For instance, whilst gaming, D observes A lying quietly beneath him listening to music. A occasionally snores whilst sleeping there, a habit shared by B and C. However, as D has frequently requested favours from A previously, D subconsciously assumes A will unconditionally comply with his demands. Consequently, A attempted to persuade C to swap sleeping spots through persistent questioning. When A refused, D employed a strategy of attrition, gradually wearing A down until they inadvertently agreed to C's request. Having no intention of relocating, A returned to find their sleeping spot changed. C then exploited cognitive dissonance by framing events from D's perspective. When A sought to discuss this with D, D merely dredged up past grievances to stir guilt, thereby successfully manipulating A's behaviour.

From the circumstances of the above case, where collusion among multiple parties led to the erosion of private sovereignty, we may draw the following conclusions:

- Pre-emptive alignment with societal expectations: Prior to the infringement, decision-maker D initiated a ‘pre-emptive alignment’ process by emphasising subject A's peculiar behaviour to the collective (B), effectively neutralising any potential intervention from B.

- Cognitive exhaustion as an obedience tool: D employed a ‘war of attrition strategy’—depleting A's cognitive reserves through persistent, high-frequency social demands. This forced A into a state of ‘unconscious compliance,’ relinquishing spatial rights due to neural fatigue rather than genuine volition.

- Post-event rationalisation and guilt induction: Following physical eviction, C reconstructed the incident through D's distorted logic, intensifying cognitive dissonance; D exploited ‘historical grievances’ to induce guilt. This dual psychological assault aimed to reshape A's cognitive framework of reality.

- A fusion of the kerosene lamp effect and cognitive restructuring: This case exemplifies the kerosene lamp effect through its use of ‘past favours’ as emotional blackmail, ‘forced takeover’ following cognitive depletion, and collusion by third party C. D's logic in dredging up past grievances is not problem-solving, but rather conveying a false signal to A: (Because you previously caused me discomfort, it is justifiable for me to cause you discomfort now – even if D's own behaviour is the source of A's dissatisfaction). By leveraging the reactions of those around them, D induces self-doubt in A, thereby trapping them in cognitive dissonance. D exploits A's ‘unintentional consent’ given in moments of exhaustion as a basis for legitimacy. When A later objects with clarity, D turns the accusation back, labelling A as “inconsistent” and sowing seeds of doubt: ‘Have I misremembered? Am I also at fault?’

As D inflicted no physical harm upon A, this resulted in A becoming trapped in an endless cycle of self-blame when attempting to resolve the issue, owing to the absence of a crucial link in the chain of evidence. Indeed, due to the intermittent reinforcement from both C and D, A developed a diagnosed case of moderate depression, which also encompassed elements of complex post-traumatic stress disorder.

From this, we can derive a model: The Paradox of Non-Physical Coercion: Structural Erasure of Evidence.

In the absence of physical aggression, subject D exploited structural vulnerabilities within the social monitoring mechanism. Through intermittent reinforcement administered by collective C, D successfully induced subject A into a state of cognitive isolation. The resulting diagnoses—moderate depression and complex post-traumatic stress disorder—were not merely symptoms of personal vulnerability, but products of a systemically toxic interactive environment. Within this environment, the burden of proof was deliberately set at an unattainable height.
