## Covert side-channel attack

This academic paper primarily focuses on demonstrating covert channel attacks, characterised by the absence of tangible evidence. Those who have experienced such incidents often find it difficult to seek redress due to insufficient evidence and the inherent challenges of forensic investigation.

To begin with, let us outline A's background. A has a particular passion for academic research. Within the family, A endured strict upbringing; specifically, due to poor academic performance during childhood, A was frequently subjected to psychological manipulation by their parents. Over time, A developed an inexpressible sense of dread towards their parents, extending to those around them. Consequently, A experiences profound anxiety in external social settings, finding it difficult to form close connections. This led to D and others forming unfavourable social perceptions of A, further exacerbating A's isolation within society.

Upon entering university, A's parents would occasionally appear unexpectedly when A was unprepared, demanding obedience. At times, A felt compelled to flee this situation. However, upon contacting A's flatmate, A's parents subsequently harassed the flatmate. To protect them, A resorted to blocking their contact details as a protective measure, yet this proved ineffective.

Moreover, when A's parents observed A failing exams, their well-intentioned desire for A to study diligently was misguided. When A faced unfair treatment at school, A's parents would only advise A to yield and avoid trouble, never teaching A how to handle such future issues or navigate interpersonal relationships.

Here, it is pertinent to reference a concept from criminal psychology describing a psychological phenomenon: A's situation aligns with the silent, reclusive type. The prominent characteristics of this personality typically manifest as: rarely displaying overtly aggressive behaviour, seldom engaging in rule-breaking activities, remaining silent for extended periods, frequently communicating through gestures in daily interactions, and often remaining indifferent to societal management and education. They express resistance through silence and may seize opportunities to act when they deem the timing ripe. Most possess introverted personalities, being solitary and quiet, with emotions rarely visible on their faces, making psychological adjustment particularly challenging.

However, the individual A in this case, despite being solitary and unreadable, possesses deep research and interests in certain specialised fields. This can be interpreted as a desire to alter their life circumstances through personal endeavour. Yet life does not operate in such a manner; in reality, effort merely increases the probability of success, not guaranteeing it. To draw an analogy with programming: life is not a program that executes automatically upon meeting conditions. Parents, lacking practical wisdom regarding A's interpersonal skills and awareness of how A should maintain personal boundaries, have inadvertently exacerbated A's reclusive tendencies and reluctance to engage with outsiders.

The parenting approach adopted by A's guardians may involve, for instance, instructing A upon arrival at school to refrain from socialising with anyone. While the parents believe this method safeguards their child from harm, within the dynamics of social interaction, such behaviour effectively reduces the system's firewall redundancy.

From the parents' operational logic, it is evident they adopt a zero-trust strategy to maximise avoidance of social risks. Yet in this society, communication remains an essential skill, and the social environment has grown more complex and volatile than ever before. When D begins to lay logical traps for A using covert, probing language, A's lack of interaction with other nodes in daily life prevents them from cross-verifying facts through multiple sources. This renders A an information silo.

Normal social standing is founded on equality. Yet the parents' behaviour may conceal a deeper motive: by creating this information silo, they aim to hinder A's integration into society, fostering an instinctive belief that home alone is safe. This further entrenches the parents' psychological control over A.

Furthermore, when A's parents instruct A to avoid socialising, they effectively deactivate A's defence mechanisms. This allows individuals like D to conduct psychological infiltration without triggering any external alerts.

According to social dynamics, a healthy system requires a degree of impurity and conflict to build resilience. A's parents' approach has established an extremely fragile safety protocol within A. Lacking experience navigating complex malicious social dynamics, A internalises signals from their parent—such as ‘your feelings don't matter’ or ‘you're causing me trouble’—when the parent feels uncomfortable. This leads A to self-negate genuine emotions. Upon encountering advanced manipulators like D, who employ intermittent reinforcement tactics, A's defences rapidly collapse into cognitive dissonance.
